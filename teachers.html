Ideas by Subject

Pick a subject, grab an idea, and run with it. Each card lists what to teach, the grade band it fits, and where to find more.

Grades 4–12

🔬 Science & Physics

  • Potential vs. kinetic energy: hold the yo-yo up (potential), throw it (kinetic), watch it sleep. The classic demo — needs five minutes and one yo-yo.
  • Newton's laws: why does the yo-yo keep spinning? Why does a tug bring it back? Friction, inertia, and angular momentum in your hand.
  • Experiment: time ten sleepers, change the string length, graph the results. Does a longer string sleep longer?

Our yo-yo science guide → · Yo-yo lesson plans on Lesson Planet ↗

Grades 3–12

🔢 Math

  • Statistics: attempt a trick 20 times, tally successes, compute the success rate. Real data, real motivation. Try our tally sheet →
  • Word problems: string length, trick counts, buying yo-yos — arithmetic with a toy attached. Print the set →
  • Geometry & algebra: the Eiffel Tower trick teaches vertical angles; the "Yo-Yo Problem" teaches linear equations (see Lesson Planet).
Grades K–6

🤸 PE & Growth Mindset

  • Hand-eye coordination: throwing and catching builds fine motor skills, bilateral coordination, and focus.
  • Growth mindset, built in: nobody lands a trick on the first try. "Never give up, encourage others, do your best" — the NED Show's whole character-education program is built on yo-yos.
  • Track progress: our trick diary printable → turns practice into a habit.

The NED Shows (school assemblies) ↗

Grades 4–12

📜 History & Culture

  • 2,500 years of history: from ancient Greece to the Philippines to Pedro Flores's 1928 factory — the yo-yo is a case study in immigration, invention, and marketing.
  • Filipino American history: Flores invented the looped string that makes every modern trick possible. Our Filipino yo-yo history page →
  • Primary sources: vintage ads, patents, and contest posters make great document-analysis exercises.

Yo-yo history guide →

Grades K–6

🎨 Art & Design

  • Coloring pages: classic and butterfly yo-yo line art, free to print. See below ↓
  • Design your own: real yo-yo companies start with sketches. Our design sheet walks kids through naming and decorating their signature yo-yo.
  • Extension: 3D-print a yo-yo at the library — see our science page → for where to print in DC/NoVA.
Grades 6–12

💻 Coding Projects

  • Real open-source templates: students can fork and customize actual club websites — a yo-yo club site, a scout troop site, a contest page. See the projects ↓
  • Skills: HTML, CSS, GitHub Pages deployment — a finished, shareable website in one class period.
