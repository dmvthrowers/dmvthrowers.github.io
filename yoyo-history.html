YO-YO HISTORY

The Story of the Yo-Yo

Ancient Greece · Pedro Flores · Duncan · Space · Today

HISTORY

From Clay Discs to World Contests

The yo-yo has been around for about 2,500 years. Here is how it got from ancient Greece to your pocket, with the DC-area chapters marked along the way. Every entry lists its sources, so you can dig deeper.

  1. c. 500 BC

    Ancient Roots

    The yo-yo is one of the oldest toys on record. Ancient Greek vase paintings show people playing with them, and surviving examples from the period are made of bronze and terracotta. The most common material was likely wood, which did not last.

    One Attic drinking cup, a kylix now in Berlin’s Antikensammlung, shows a boy playing with a yo-yo. Nobody knows what the Greeks called it.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · The Engines of Our Ingenuity: The Yo-Yo · Duncan: A Rich History of a Simple Toy

  2. 1700s

    Around the World as the Bandalore

    A Mughal painting from northern India, around 1770, shows a woman with a string toy similar to the modern yo-yo.

    By the end of the 1700s the toy was popular in England, where people called it a bandalore or bandylore. A 1791 illustration shows a woman playing one. The French knew it as the joujou de Normandie.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · YoYo Wiki: History of the Yo-yo

  3. The Name

    A Word from the Philippines

    The word yo-yo most likely comes from the Ilocano word yóyo, or a related word from the Philippines. The Strong National Museum of Play translates it as “come come.” Filipino children played with the toy for generations before it reached America.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · The Strong: Duncan Yo-Yo

  4. 1866

    American Patents

    U.S. patents for yo-yo-like toys date to the 1860s. An 1866 patent by James L. Haven and Charles Hettrick described a new way to build one around a central rivet. In America the toy was still called a whirligig or bandalore.

    Sources: YoYo Wiki: History of the Yo-yo · The Engines of Our Ingenuity: The Yo-Yo

  5. 1928

    Pedro Flores Makes the Yo-Yo Sleep

    Pedro Flores, a Filipino immigrant, opened the Yo-yo Manufacturing Company in Santa Barbara, California, in 1928. It started with a dozen handmade toys. By November 1929 he ran two more factories in Los Angeles and Hollywood, with 600 workers turning out 300,000 yo-yos a day.

    His key change was the string. Flores used one continuous loop around the axle instead of tying it tight. That let the yo-yo spin freely at the end of the string, or “sleep,” the move nearly every trick is built on.

    DC Connection Read the full story, from exhibit banners by Robert Reyes, on our Filipino Yo-Yo History page.

    Sources: Pedro Flores (Wikipedia) · Pedro and the Yo-Yo: Man and Myth · The Engines of Our Ingenuity: The Yo-Yo · Our Filipino Yo-Yo History page

  6. 1929 – 1932

    Duncan and the First Craze

    Businessman Donald F. Duncan saw Flores’s yo-yos and bought the company, taking over the Flores name by 1932. Duncan partnered with newspaper owner William Randolph Hearst: kids entered yo-yo contests by selling newspaper subscriptions. Duncan says the Chicago paper alone sold 50,000 new subscriptions in six weeks.

    Duncan then sent teams of demonstrators, many of them Filipino, from city to city and schoolyard to schoolyard to teach tricks and run contests.

    DC Connection The Smithsonian’s National Museum of American History, right here in DC, holds the Duncan Family Yo-Yo Collection. It includes 35 photographs of demonstrator Tommy Reyes, donated by his son Robert in 2017.

    Sources: Donald F. Duncan Sr. (Wikipedia) · Duncan Toys Company (Wikipedia) · Duncan: A Rich History of a Simple Toy · Oak Park River Forest Museum: Donald Duncan · Smithsonian: Duncan Family Yo-Yo Collection

  7. 1932

    The First World Contest

    The first World Yo-Yo Contest was held in London in 1932. Harvey Lowe, a 13-year-old from Victoria, British Columbia, won it at the Empire Theatre. He went on to master more than 2,000 tricks.

    Sources: Harvey Lowe (Wikipedia) · World Yo-Yo Contest (Wikipedia)

  8. 1946 – 1960s

    Factories, TV, and Tricks on Screen

    In 1946 Duncan opened a yo-yo factory in Luck, Wisconsin. Sales dipped after World War II, so in 1962 Duncan launched a comeback with television ads and the slogan “If it isn’t a Duncan, it isn’t a yo-yo.”

    On TV, Tom Smothers created his “Yo-Yo Man” character, and Harvey Lowe appeared on The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour as his guru.

    DC Connection Duncan champion Tommy Reyes did yo-yo spots on Washington and Baltimore kids’ TV, including Bozo with Willard Scott, who played Bozo on WRC-TV in DC from 1959 to 1962.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · The Strong: Duncan Yo-Yo · Harvey Lowe (Wikipedia) · Willard Scott (Wikipedia) · Our Filipino Yo-Yo History page

  9. 1965 – 1968

    “Yo-Yo” Becomes Everyone’s Word

    Duncan sued Royal Tops, a company started by former Duncan demonstrator Joe Radovan, over the word “yo-yo.” In 1965 a federal appeals court ruled the word had become part of common speech, so Duncan no longer owned it.

    Legal costs and other pressures led the Duncan family to sell the company name in 1968 to Flambeau, which had made Duncan’s plastic yo-yos since 1955.

    Sources: Duncan v. Royal Tops (7th Cir. 1965) · Museum of Yo-Yo History: Royal · Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · Duncan Toys Company (Wikipedia)

  10. 1970s – 1990

    Better Axles, Longer Spins

    Dentist Tom Kuhn, missing the wooden yo-yos of his youth, built the “No-Jive” yo-yo in the 1970s. Swedish bearing maker SKF made novelty ball-bearing yo-yos in 1984. In 1990 Kuhn introduced the SB-2, with an aluminum transaxle, the first successful ball-bearing yo-yo.

    Bearings cut friction dramatically. A cheap fixed-axle yo-yo spins 10 to 20 seconds; a good bearing yo-yo can spin for minutes.

    Sources: Tom Kuhn (Wikipedia) · Yo-yo (Wikipedia)

  11. 1985

    Yo-Yos in Space

    In April 1985 the crew of Space Shuttle Discovery, mission STS-51D, flew a small collection of toys, including a yo-yo, a Slinky, a paddle ball, and a toy car. They filmed the toys in weightlessness as a physics lesson for students.

    DC Connection The Smithsonian’s National Air and Space Museum, on the National Mall, tells the Toys in Space story.

    Sources: Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum: Toys in Space · NASA: STS-51D · Duncan: A Rich History of a Simple Toy

  12. 1990s

    The Big Boom

    Dale Oliver restarted the World Yo-Yo Contest as an annual event in 1992, at the International Jugglers’ Association convention in Montreal. It moved to Hawaii in 1999 and to Orlando in 2000.

    A worldwide yo-yo boom peaked in the late 1990s. In 1999 the Duncan yo-yo was inducted into the National Toy Hall of Fame, and Steve Brown debuted freehand (5A) play, with the string tied to a counterweight instead of a finger.

    Sources: World Yo-Yo Contest (Wikipedia) · Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · National Toy Hall of Fame (Wikipedia) · The Strong: Duncan Yo-Yo · Steve Brown (Wikipedia)

  13. 2000s

    The Modern Era

    After the boom came what players call the modern era. Wider gaps, silicone response pads, and the bind trick made unresponsive yo-yos possible. With the yo-yo no longer coming back on a tug, players invented a huge new vocabulary of string tricks.

    The National Yo-Yo Hall of Fame began in 2003, inducting Pedro Flores, Donald F. Duncan Sr., and Barney Akers.

    DC Connection Springfield, VA performer Dick Stohr, “That Yo-Yo Guy,” played the National Theatre in DC in 2003. The Virginia State Yo-Yo Contest was first held on January 21, 2006. In 2010, 2,036 people set the Guinness record for most people yo-yoing at once at the National Scout Jamboree at Fort A.P. Hill, VA.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · YoYo Wiki: National Yo-Yo Hall of Fame · YoYo Wiki: Dick Stohr · YoYo Wiki: US State Contest Virginia · Guinness World Records

  14. 2013 – Today

    A Global Sport

    In 2013 the International Yo-Yo Federation (IYYF) formed to run the World Yo-Yo Contest, which now rotates between the Americas, Europe, and Asia. After online contests in 2021 and 2022, Worlds returned in Osaka in 2023, then Cleveland in 2024, Prague in 2025, and Himeji, Japan in 2026.

    In the U.S., the National Yo-Yo League runs contests across nine regions. Maryland and Virginia sit in the Mid-Atlantic region. The Hall of Fame restarted in 2020 under Doc Lucky Meisenheimer, and in 2025 it inducted Filipino Duncan champion Tommy Reyes.

    Sources: International Yo-Yo Federation · World Yo-Yo Contest · World Yo-Yo Contest (Wikipedia) · National Yo-Yo League · YoYo Wiki: National Yo-Yo League · National Yo-Yo Hall of Fame roster

  15. Today

    Where the Yo-Yo Is Now

    Players learn from free video tutorials and trick apps, and share tricks on Instagram with the #trickcircle hashtag, on YouTube, and on TikTok. Duncan, still based in Middlefield, Ohio, became an independent company again in 2024. National Yo-Yo Day is June 6, Donald Duncan’s birthday.

    DC Connection DMV Throwers meets free every month in Arlington, and the Virginia State Yo-Yo Contest came to Dulles Town Center in 2026. Pick up a loaner and start your own chapter of the story.

    Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia) · Duncan Toys Company (Wikipedia) · Donald F. Duncan Sr. (Wikipedia)

The Styles of Play

Contests group tricks into divisions by style:

  • 1A String tricks. One unresponsive yo-yo. The most popular style.
  • 2A Looping. Two responsive yo-yos looping around the hands.
  • 3A Two-handed string tricks. Two unresponsive yo-yos at once.
  • 4A Off-string. The yo-yo is not attached to the string and is thrown into the air.
  • 5A Freehand. The string ends in a counterweight instead of a finger loop.
  • 0A Modern responsive. One responsive yo-yo with stalls and skateboard-style tricks.

Sources: Yo-yo (Wikipedia)
