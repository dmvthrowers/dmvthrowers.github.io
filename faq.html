Frequently Asked Questions

Got a question? Chances are it's answered below. If not, reach us at contact@dmvthrowers.club or @DMV_THROWERS on Instagram.

General
DMV Throwers is a free, community-run yo-yo and skill toy club serving the Washington DC, Maryland, and Virginia area. We bring together throwers of all ages and experience levels, from total beginners to national competitors. Our goal is simple: build a fun, welcoming community around yo-yo and skill toys.
The club was founded by Arthur Foley (President/Founder) and is coordinated day-to-day by Brandon Rogers (Vice President). Our current officer team also includes Abby Brodsky (Co-Coordinator). We're a volunteer-run organization.
No. Attending our events is completely free. We accept voluntary donations if you'd like to support the club, but there are no required fees, dues, or memberships of any kind. DMV Throwers is not a registered nonprofit, so donations are not tax-deductible.

Meetup Logistics
Monthly meetups are held at Arlington Central Library, Barbara M. Donnellan Auditorium / Bluemont Room, 1015 N Quincy St, Arlington, VA 22201, every 3rd Sunday from 1–4 PM. Anytime in that window works. People come and go throughout. If it's your first time, arriving a little earlier gives you more time to get settled. During warmer months we sometimes hold outdoor meetups at Quincy Park in Arlington, VA.
For regular monthly meetups, no registration required. Just show up. For special events, an RSVP may be requested and will be noted in the event announcement. If a meetup is ever cancelled, it will be announced as early as possible on Instagram (@DMV_THROWERS) and our Facebook page. Always check social media close to the date if you're unsure.
Yes. Street parking is available on N Quincy St and surrounding streets. The library is about a 5-minute walk from the Virginia Square–GMU Metro station on the Orange and Silver lines.

Games, Challenges & Teaching
Lots, and most need no special skill. These come from a longtime club founder on the YoYoExpert Forums: Dragster Races (throw a sleeper, slip the string off your finger, and let the yo-yo race across the floor), Closest to the Edge (slide your yo-yo across a table and stop it nearest the edge without falling off; pad the floor), and sleeper challenges (how many times can you run around your throwing hand, or how many fist bumps can you give, before the yo-yo stops?). Ending with a group game is the best way to make people want to come back.
A fun, informal contest. One format from the YoYoExpert Forums: put everyone’s name in a hat, draw two at a time, and give each player two tries to impress three judges. Winners move on until one is left. The prize yo-yo gets “dinged” by everyone first, so it carries the whole group’s marks.
Try a classic-trick month. A January 2026 forum challenge picked 10 old-school tricks: Around the Corner, Rock the Baby, Stop and Go, Pinwheels, Eiffel Tower, Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, Motorcycle, One-Handed Star, Rock the Baby in the Eiffel Tower, and Frog in a Bag. The forums also run style months like Fixed Axle February and 5A May. See Keep Learning.
Make progress visible. One parent on the YoYoExpert Forums taped a big beginner trick checklist to the wall for their 7-year-old and friends. Print our Beginner Trick Checklist, start with a responsive yo-yo, and bring them to a meetup, where loaners and other kids make it easier.
Advice from people who have started clubs on the YoYoExpert Forums: have inexpensive take-apart yo-yos, both responsive and unresponsive, plus lots of string (4 to 5 per new player). Train a few helpers, because beginners have lots of problems in the first 30 minutes. Meet at a consistent time and place, welcome each person by name, and mark a yo-yo zone with tape or cones so no one gets bonked. Plan about 30 minutes for young kids and about an hour for adults. In 2019, YoYoExpert also announced a Club Grant Program with starter kits and leveled trick cards; check with them for its current status. Need a website? Our free yo-yo club website template gets you one in about 30 minutes, and there’s a contest template too. We’re happy to help, too: email contact@dmvthrowers.club about a workshop.
