History

  • National Yo-Yo MuseumThe world's largest public yo-yo display. Free to visit in Chico, CA.
  • Doc Lucky's History of the Yo-YoFrom ancient Greece to the modern contest scene.
  • Smithsonian: Yo-Yo HeritageThe National Museum of American History, right here in DC, holds the Duncan company archives.
  • Duncan Family Yo-Yo CollectionThe Duncan family's yo-yo collection, preserved in the archives of the National Museum of American History in DC. It includes 35 photographs of Tommy Reyes, donated by his son Robert in 2017.
  • Bob Rule PapersSmithsonian archive of a Hall of Fame yo-yo and top demonstrator, including a file on Filipino demonstrator Gus Somera.
  • The Legacy of Tommy ReyesFilipino American Duncan champion of 1932 who later did yo-yo spots on DC and Baltimore kids' TV, including Bozo with Willard Scott. National Yo-Yo Hall of Fame. Read the exhibit.
  • Dick Stohr, "That Yo-Yo Guy"Springfield, VA performer, six-time world record setter, and longtime contest judge. He played the National Theatre in DC in 2003. Profiled in The Washington Post. More on the YoYo Wiki.
  • Ann Connolly (Bunake)DC-raised and one of the best-known women in yo-yo since the early 2000s. World Contest finalist. Learn her tricks on YoYoTricks.com.
  • Tom ConnollyAnn's dad, spin top standout, and collector with a massive collection. Watch him at the 2012 World Spin Top Competition.
  • Virginia State Contest HistoryVA States first ran on January 21, 2006. The first results include Ann and Tom Connolly. VSYC-26 carries it forward.
  • Jonathan Robinson (JonRob)5A player from Lynchburg, VA, and 2007 Trick Innovator of the Year at U.S. Nationals.
  • Mikhail TulabutVirginia string-trick innovator, Team YoYoJam member, and co-founder of Southeast Virginia Skillz Club (2005).
  • World Record at Fort A.P. HillThe Guinness record for most people yo-yoing at once: 2,036 people at the 2010 National Scout Jamboree in Caroline County, VA.
  • Capital YoYo ClubAn earlier DC, MD, and NoVA club. Its forum thread is a snapshot of local yo-yo history. The YoYo Wiki notes many of its members now throw with DMV Throwers.
  • Pedro FloresThe Filipino American maker who brought the yo-yo to the U.S. in the 1920s.
  • Pedro and the Yo-Yo: Man and MythPositively Filipino on Flores and the Filipino demonstrators who carried the 1930s craze.
  • The Yoyo: A Story of Downs and UpsPositively Filipino profile of Fred Urian, one of the Filipino demonstrators the Flores brothers trained in Chicago in 1932.
  • Royal Tops and the Word “Yo-Yo”Filipino demonstrators Joe Radovan and Ruben Delagana founded Royal, which won the 1965 case that made “yo-yo” a common word.
  • Donald F. Duncan Sr.The businessman who bought Flores's company and sent demonstrator teams across the country. National Yo-Yo Day, June 6, is his birthday.
  • The Engines of Our Ingenuity: The Yo-YoA short University of Houston radio essay on Flores, Duncan, and who deserves credit for the yo-yo.
  • Yo-Yo Man (1978)Duncan's documentary featuring demonstrator Nemo Concepcion, one of the Filipino pros from the 1930s boom.
  • National Yo-Yo Hall of Fame VideosDoc Lucky's profiles of Hall of Fame members and nominees.
  • Yo-Yo on WikipediaA solid overview of the toy, its booms, and its players.
  • YoYo WikiFan-built wiki of players, companies, contests, and tricks.
