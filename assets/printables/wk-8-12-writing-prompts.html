Yo-Yo Writing Prompts

Choose one prompt (or one assigned by your teacher). Write at least three paragraphs. Use specific details — no filler.

Name: ______________________________Date: ____________

1. Describe, step by step, the first time you learned a yo-yo trick — or watched someone learn one. What went wrong before it went right?Narrative

2. Explain exactly how a yo-yo "sleeps" at the bottom of the string and how a tug brings it back. Write so that someone who has never touched a yo-yo could understand.Informative

3. Should schools spend money on skill-toy programs (yo-yos, kendamas, juggling) for PE or after-school clubs? Make your case with at least two reasons and one counterargument you answer.Argumentative

4. Pedro Flores, a Filipino immigrant, invented the looped string that makes modern yo-yo tricks possible; Donald Duncan bought Flores's company and made the yo-yo famous worldwide. Who deserves more credit for the yo-yo — the inventor or the marketer? Research both men, cite at least two sources, and defend your answer.Research

5. Compare learning a yo-yo trick to learning any other hard skill (an instrument, a sport, a language). What's the same about the process? What's different?Compare / Contrast

6. You are designing a yo-yo for a contest. Describe your design — shape, weight, materials, colors — and explain why each choice would help a player win.Descriptive

7. "A yo-yo always comes back." Write about a time you (or someone you know) kept trying at something that kept failing — and what finally changed.Reflective

8. In 1965 a court ruled the word "yo-yo" had become generic, and Duncan lost its trademark. Was that the right call? Consider both the company that built the brand and the competitors who wanted to use the word.Argumentative

✂ Teacher Notes — Quick 4-Point Rubric (cut or fold under before copying)
Category4 — Strong3 — Good2 — Developing1 — Beginning
IdeasClear, original thinking; specific details throughoutClear ideas with some specific detailsIdeas present but vague or repetitiveIdeas unclear or off-prompt
OrganizationLogical flow; strong opening and closingMostly organized; paragraphs in sensible orderWeak structure; jumps aroundNo clear structure
EvidenceFacts/examples well chosen and cited (#4, #8)Some supporting detail or sourcesLittle support; mostly opinionNo support given
ConventionsCorrect grammar, spelling, punctuationA few minor errorsErrors distract the readerErrors block meaning

Discussion points: #4 — there is no single right answer; look for use of sources over vibes. #8 — good answers weigh both sides (brand investment vs. language belonging to everyone). #3 — strong answers address cost and the counterargument that PE time is already scarce.
